# Catalytic Mechanism Map: Candida antarctica Lipase B (CALB)

## Introduction

Candida antarctica Lipase B (CALB) is an industrially
important lipase belonging to the α/β-hydrolase family.

## Recent Research Article Review

Five recent research articles were reviewed to understand the
catalytic behaviour, active-site environment, immobilization,
enzyme engineering, and industrial applications of
Candida antarctica Lipase B (CALB).

| No. | Research Article / Research Focus | Year | Key Finding |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | CALB activity in water–organic solvent mixtures | 2023 | The solvent environment influenced the active-site structure, catalytic triad and enzyme activity. |
| 2 | Engineering of CALB for enhanced perhydrolysis activity | 2024 | Modification of the potential water channel improved access of hydrogen peroxide to the active site and affected catalytic performance. |
| 3 | Development of CALB-based biocatalysts using porous polymer supports | 2024/2025 | Immobilization on porous supports was investigated to improve enzyme stability and catalytic usability. |
| 4 | Immobilized CALB for biodiesel production from rapeseed oil | 2025 | Immobilized CALB was evaluated for biodiesel production, with immobilization and reaction conditions affecting catalytic performance. |
| 5 | CALB immobilized on mesoporous MIL-88A for phospholipid synthesis | 2025 | Immobilized CALB showed high catalytic efficiency for incorporation of EPA/DHA into phosphatidylcholine. |

### Overall Literature Findings

The recent studies show that CALB catalytic performance is
strongly influenced by the active-site environment, substrate
accessibility, solvent conditions and enzyme immobilization.

These studies support the importance of the catalytic triad
*Ser105-Asp187-His224* and the oxyanion-hole residues
*Thr40 and Gln106* in CALB catalysis.

## Active-Site Residues

| Residue | Function |

## Catalytic Mechanism

1. Substrate binding
2. Ser105 nucleophilic attack
3. Tetrahedral intermediate formation
4. Oxyanion stabilization
5. Acyl-enzyme formation
6. Water/alcohol attack
7. Product release
8. Enzyme regeneration

# Industrial Applications

CALB is an important industrial enzyme because of its high
stability, broad substrate specificity and excellent
stereoselectivity.

Biodiesel Production: CALB catalyzes transesterification reactions for producing biodiesel from oils and alcohols.

Pharmaceutical Synthesis: CALB is used for the
  enantioselective synthesis and resolution of pharmaceutical
  intermediates.

Flavor and Fragrance Production: CALB catalyzes ester
  synthesis used in flavor and fragrance industries.

Food Processing: CALB is used in the modification and
  synthesis of food-related lipids and esters.

Green Chemistry: CALB enables selective reactions under
  relatively mild conditions and can reduce the use of harsh
  chemical catalysts.

## Conclusion

Candida antarctica Lipase B (CALB) is an industrially important
lipase that uses the Ser105-Asp187-His224 catalytic triad for
ester hydrolysis and transesterification. The oxyanion hole,
formed mainly by Thr40 and Gln106, helps stabilize the reaction
intermediate. Understanding these active-site residues and
catalytic steps helps improve the use of CALB in sustainable
industrial biocatalysis.
